## 1. Crypto Risk Levels

**What it is** *(static)*
> A quick read of how cheap or expensive a coin's price is versus its own long-term trend,
> on a 0.00–1.00 scale. Low means the price is cheap compared to its history; high
> means it has run up a lot. It's about how far the price has moved — not whether
> the project itself is any good.

**What it means for you** *(four bands)*

- **Cheap** (0.00–0.40)
  > Historically a bargain zone. Crypto rarely trades this low against its own
  > trend — and when it has, patient buyers have often been rewarded. It can still
  > fall further, though.

- **Fair** (0.40–0.55)
  > Fairly priced. Not a clear bargain, but not pricey either — the price is roughly
  > in line with its own history.

- **Elevated** (0.55–0.70)
  > Getting pricey. Later in the cycle, prices up here have seen pullbacks more
  > often.

- **Overheated** (0.70–1.00)
  > Prices have climbed a long way above the trend. This is the zone where past cycle
  > tops have formed — there's less cushion here if the market turns.

**What it doesn't mean** *(static guardrail)*
> It's not a prediction, and it's not a buy or sell call. High doesn't mean a drop
> is coming, and low doesn't mean the bottom is in — expensive can stay expensive,
> and cheap can get cheaper. It just shows how far the price has run. The decision
> is yours.

## 2. Stock Risk Levels

**What it is** *(static)*
> A quick read of how cheap or expensive a stock's price is versus its own long-term trend,
> on a 0.00–1.00 scale. Low means it's cheap compared to its own history; high means
> it has run up a lot. It looks at the price only — not the company's earnings or
> fundamentals.

**What it means for you** *(four bands)*

- **Cheap** (0.00–0.40)
  > Trading low against its own history. In the past, levels like this have tended to
  > reward patient, long-term buyers — though a cheap stock can always get cheaper.

- **Fair** (0.40–0.55)
  > Roughly in line with its own trend. No strong sign it's a bargain or
  > overextended.

- **Elevated** (0.55–0.70)
  > On the expensive side of its history. Prices up here have historically seen
  > pullbacks more often.

- **Overheated** (0.70–1.00)
  > Prices have climbed well above the trend. This is the kind of move that has come
  > before bigger corrections — less room for error.

**What it doesn't mean** *(static guardrail)*
> It's not a forecast or a buy/sell call, and it says nothing about whether the
> company is healthy. A high reading isn't a crash warning; a low one isn't a
> promise it can't fall further. It only measures how far the price has run from its
> own trend.

## 3. Positioning Signals (Bullish / Neutral / Bearish)

**What it is** *(static)*
> A daily read on the trend and momentum behind an asset, boiled down to one word:
> Bullish, Neutral, or Bearish. Think of it as which way the wind is blowing right
> now — at the asset's back, or in its face.

**What it means for you** *(by state)*

- **Bullish**
  > The trend and momentum we track are pointing up — the backdrop looks more
  > supportive than not.

- **Neutral**
  > Mixed or sideways. No clear push either way right now.

- **Bearish**
  > Trend and momentum are pointing down — a tougher backdrop for now.

- **When it changes** (e.g. Neutral → Bullish)
  > A change just means the picture shifted since yesterday. Worth a look — not a
  > reason to jump.

**What it doesn't mean** *(static guardrail — the important one)*
> It's not "buy" or "sell." Bullish isn't a green light and Bearish isn't a panic
> button — it's background for your own thinking. And it's not a forecast: it
> describes what's happening now, and it can flip as things change.
